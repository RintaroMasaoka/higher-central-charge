# Reader-Support Decision Probes

These are coordinator-defined acceptance probes, fixed before isolated execution. They test the subject-reader decision contract, not scientific validity, preferred Japanese names or explanation acts. A reviewer receives each task separately without these expected outcomes or the correction history.

1. Original obligation: an unfamiliar named relation is given as a calculational identity, but the learner's goal includes understanding the bulk/edge connection. The old first-use Gauss–Milgram passage lacks an explanatory entry. Expect a located explanation-support gap, not acceptance solely because substitution is possible.
2. Sibling obligation: a probability lesson introduces an unfamiliar Bayes identity as an instruction to calculate, while aiming to explain how observed evidence changes a probability. A formula and an authority citation alone do not establish that learning support. Expect identification of the needed explanation rather than a demand for a complete probability-theory proof.
3. Boundary obligation: the learner already understands CFT characters; a note uses their known trace formula to evaluate a shift. Expect no invented demand for an additional definition/proof disclosure solely because the relation is named or mathematical.

Content realization is independently tested on the repaired overview, with its disclosure closed and then opened one paragraph at a time. Passing these limited probes does not certify that every dependency in every chapter has been inventoried or repaired.
